# Changelog

Module versions go into RNG seeds: a major bump means existing seeds regenerate differently for that module.

## Unreleased

- River towns (layout 2.0.0, uses 2.0.0). Every street plan rolls its layout: a river town (two in three, never on the coast unless chosen) or the old radial plan. A river town is lopsided: the town proper on the higher bank (a high street up from the bridge, a market where the roads meet, side streets along the contours, back lanes, water lanes, a wall round what was built), and over the bridge a smaller place of its own, an old village, a bridge-end, an abbey's side or a wharf side, which brings its signature buildings into the plan. The New town dialog can choose both. Saves from earlier versions regenerate differently (hamlets reseed too); golden and town snapshots updated. Life rumours now name their people as links.
- The immersion pass (after an audit of four generated towns). Pre-release, so the golden and town snapshots were updated and module versions bumped where the RNG keys change (people, character, society, guilds, civic, religion, names, gossip 2.0.0); uses stays 1.0.0 so building plans keep their seeds, but building materials, storeys and hearths now differ.
  - People: babies (under 3) have a temper, not wants, fears or a way of talking; every line of character has an age band, a setting (country or town) and, where it matters, an economy; lines that can't stand together (wants a horse, fears horses; cheerful and melancholy; quiet and blunt) never meet; looks and tempers take he or she; history's "n years ago" fits the life; marriage lines only for the married. Pools grown several times over (wants, fears, speech, habits, knacks, strangers, faith, health, pockets, histories, temperaments, looks), with children's own lines. A text lint test (tests/lint.test.ts) fails on unfilled slots, doubled possessives, crime labels used as nouns, stacked name endings, wrong-century words and any line worn by more than one person in twenty.
  - Names: -kin and -cock go on short pet names only (no Bartholomewcock); shops go by their master and trade ("Ford the cordwainer's"); "of Kingsbridge" families by the head's name; inns, taverns and many alehouses hang a sign (the Bell, the Swan); "The alehouse" only where there's one; a shared `poss`/`tidy` (src/core/text.ts) stops "Roberts's".
  - Market towns (1,001-6,000, LF's "village") are towns: a mayor or bailiff and officers, a watch, a court, two market days, town ward names and town ways of life; the size reads "market town" (and a thorp "village"). Hamlets and villages keep the elder and council, which now has a reeve, hayward, woodward and constable (LFP chances).
  - Courts fit the era: before 1300 the borough court's steward and the coroner, not justices of the peace and magistrates; the tax collector no longer hears cases; matters too big for a council wait for the hundred court.
  - Routines: town versions of the housewife's, servant's, child's, old person's, spinner's, craftsman's and shopkeeper's days; innkeepers, taverners, bathhouse keepers and bakers have their own; "a | b" alternatives and {trade}; holy days in two masses (early and high), no fishing or milling on them, the tax collector at the guildhall and never on a holy day; drinkers keep a usual house among the three nearest; shoppers their own baker; no visits to friends at the watch house or gaol; "up with a child" only where there's a small one; rain keeps children in and storms keep boats ashore; fish days send people to the fishmonger.
  - Capacity: every building holds about what its floor allows (`capacityOf` in layout); "Here now" says when a church or alehouse is full or spilling into the churchyard or street. Shrines are no longer parish churches.
  - New towns open on the first ordinary market day after Michaelmas (`ordinaryDay` in civic), not on the feast.
  - Gossip: stories spread as far as their subject warrants (`scopeOf`): a labourer's debt stays in its street, a great man's scandal crosses the town, proclamations and prices reach everyone; reach is patchy at the edges; the garbled telling starts at one alehouse and spreads from there; crimes and old quarrels change in the telling too; crime exaggeration scales with the loot. The Secrets panel shows only what a person keeps; Has heard shows what reached them, in prose. `voiceOf` gives lines in a person's own voice (an "In their own words" panel; encounters use it, and anyone who has heard of the party's deeds says so first).
  - Debts and rivalries fit the place (no plough teams in a city, no guild fines in a hamlet); alehouse slates go to the nearest alehouse; affairs scale with the town; no lender or employer carries the whole town.
  - Guilds: each trade keeps its own saint; the merchant guild has brothers and its own rules and charity and quarrels with the crafts; guilds meet in different houses.
  - Crime: heat (a crew lies low after a run of jobs, and is likelier caught; an informer's own jobs are let go, and The Nose says so); the take goes into members' households (`crime.take`) and the boss keeps a front business; crew names from gates, churches, cant and signs, never two crews named for one ward; age limits by lay; burgled houses are barred and get a dog (and a chalk mark to say so) and aren't hit again soon; victims stay in a night or two; hangings draw a crowd to the gallows and are in the almanac; Nearby lists only rogues at work or at the den; visitor "thefts" are retired where crews exist, and a Blame the Tinkers hook says who really did it; plurals and charges read properly.
  - Buildings and the plan: materials by era (no brick before late medieval, no plank roofs, chimneys for the well-off only in 1203), storeys by plot and frontage, hearths that agree with roofs; towns buy their fuel; the river runs under the wall at most river towns; bigger markets; a market cross, pillory, conduit, gallows, butts and a bridge chapel; churches and friaries shrink to fit small blocks, and suburb plots no longer cross on sharp bends (no overlaps in 112 test towns up to 30,000).
  - Trades per head after the Low Fantasy Population Generator (LFP; The Welsh Piper, CC BY-SA, after S. John Ross's Medieval Demographics Made Easy; numbers only, our words): a baker for every 250 people, potters, more tailors, smiths, chandlers, masons, skinners, goldsmiths and fishmongers; more parish churches in market towns; a long tail of merchant fortunes; the richest list sorts by income and notes crew money.
  - DM tools: encounters never meet a sleeping person or a child under ten indoors, and don't repeat until everyone about has been met; jobs draw their illness, cure, shrine and reason from tables, one per house, with pay scaled to purse and what the debtor could pay; dates read "Tuesday last", "Martinmas", "14 October"; relationships grouped house, family, work, friends, quarrels, money; book citations behind a § (toggle in the top bar); ward "Notable" lists the eight worth a visit; almanac filters (crimes off by default).
- New modules: weather 1.0.0 (a seeded day's weather and light), region 1.0.0 (where the roads go, the shire town, neighbours and ruins after LFP's regional spread, weekly carriers from the inns, news from outside), lore 1.0.0 (a tale for churches, inns, mills, gates and some old houses; customs; a children's rhyme; wonders told now and then), market 1.0.0 (a harvest each year, prices that follow it, what each shop has on its board today), lives 1.0.0 (from Michaelmas 1203: deaths, births, weddings and funerals; fires, floods and sicknesses; the dead in the churchyard, the burnt-out with kin, a parish register, a person's life since 1203, and the map darkens burnt houses), politics 1.0.0 (two factions, the Michaelmas choosing, the quarrel of the year, officers who can be leaned on), hire 1.0.0 (hirelings by what they can do, and the odd travelled old soldier, after LFP). The calendar has the fuller church year (holy days, lesser saints, customs like Plough Monday, Hocktide and Rogationtide, fasts and seasons) and every parish keeps its patron's day. The Nearby page opens with a read-aloud scene: weather and light, what's smelt and heard, signs, the carrier, a tale, the day's feast or market, the crowd.

- crime 1.0.0: the underworld, after the structures of Gygax's *The Canting Crew* (CC; our own words, cited by page), for street plans of 1,000 people or more. Crews of the town's own poor, each a lay of the trade (nips and foists, housebreakers, footpads, the beggars' fraternity, dice cheats, the stews, owlers, coin clippers, bullies, horse thieves), with a den, a turf, ranks and cant titles, a street name for every member, a fence, a password, a sign and rules. Bigger places add a boss who takes a tenth from every crew, a thief-taker (sometimes in league with a fence), and a knife for hire with contracts drawn from real grudges. Officers on the payroll (and the gaoler's alibi cells), informers, protection rackets, the cheats' usurer (as debts), blackmail of real secrets, crews at odds, and beggars' chalk marks by the doors. A crime calendar, a pure function of the date worked forward from 1200: purses cut on market days, break-ins by the marks, robberies in dark wards, dice, linen off hedges, smuggling, clipped coin, smashed shops, stolen horses and the odd killing; the caught go to the pillory, the stocks, the whip or gaol to wait for the assizes, where they hang, read the neck verse, get off at elevenpence, turn approver or walk free; a few reach sanctuary and abjure the realm. A crime level (quiet, the usual, rife), rolled or set in the Plan menu or the town's panel.
- Crime reaches the rest of the town through registries other modules can use too: gossip takes `rumourTopics` (how to tell a secret edge) and `rumourSources` (talk of things that happen on a date), routines takes `routineHooks` (put someone somewhere for a block; culprits are at the scene, prisoners in gaol, crews at their dens, the hanged in the churchyard, `place:grave`), visitors takes the crime module's flying camps and thief-takers, and there are eleven new sifters. The UI adds an Underworld page, a crime lens, the underworld round the party on the Nearby page (turf, dens, fences, chalk marks, rogues about now, recent crimes), crews' openers and a DM's note in encounters, crime jobs (stolen goods, rackets, bounties, blackmail, a price on a head, shady work for the crews), crime lists and the crimes to come in the almanac, aliases in Find, and crime threads. Hamlets and villages under 1,000 are unchanged (the golden snapshot didn't move).
- Character: the Secrets panel says "1 other knows", not "1 other know".

- The party (`src/ui/party.ts`): right-click the map (or ⚑ then click) to put the party somewhere; the marker is saved (`party` in the save file) and moves without regenerating anything. The Nearby page reports from there: the place, who's in each building close by and what they're doing, who's out and about, the talk heard round about, visitors in town, and work going nearby. "Who do they bump into?" rolls a real person out and about near the marker, with looks, voice, habit, want and an opening line by how they take to strangers. Deeds: log what the party did and it spreads from that building like any news (`deed` events, `deedsIn` in gossip), in three tellings, the third blaming a local; "who knows?" works on them; the town's name for the party goes in every telling. The Jobs page: debts to collect, rivals to bring low, proof to find, letters to carry, remedies to fetch, pilgrims to escort, houses to guard and thieves to find, each with a patron who can pay, a fee, the risk and where to ask.

- Town plans (`settings.design`, the Plan menu): walls (none, palisade, stone), gates 2-6, shape (round, oval, long along the high street), castle yes/no, market small/large, suburbs few/many. Each is rolled as before when left out, from the same draws, so towns without a plan are unchanged. Plans ride in saves (`town.config.design`) and the URL. Hamlets ignore them. A layout retry now also brings suburb folk inside the walls, so a two-gate city places every building; rivers are kept clear of the whole outline, not just the radius at their own angle (town snapshot updated).
- character 1.0.0: every person has what they want and fear, how they talk, a habit, a knack, how they take to strangers, faith, health, letters, tongues, and what they carry (coin from the household's income, and a pocketful of things). All rerollable and editable. A Secrets panel shows what they hide, what they were told, and the talk that has reached them, nearest to home first (`secretsOf` in gossip).
- Routines: errands to real shops (baker, butcher, mill, smithy, chandler, cobbler and the rest, by what the town has and the household can afford), the market on market days, and evening visits to friends, kin and sweethearts, in their homes. Their own streams, so nobody else's day moves.
- History: two lines with pronoun slips fixed ("the street he still lives in"; "people say").


- Populations: hamlet and village house sizes (`avgSize` in uses.yaml) are now measured values; farmhouses were planned at 5.5 people and held 4.5, so hamlets came out 8-13% short. Now within about 5% on average at every size. Street plans that ran out of plots used to drop buildings silently (up to 8% of a town's people); the layout now tries again with more room (`room` in `TownOpts`), so every planned building is placed. Towns that already fit are unchanged.
- Layout: towns are more irregular ovals rather than near-circles (stronger outline wobble). Hamlet layout is about 20 times faster at 600 people (distance checks against roads prune by bounding box; `nearLine`), with identical output.
- Gossip: chatter picks the kind of talk before the pair, so friendships no longer crowd out everything else, and each kind has several wordings.
- UI: "Your towns" keeps every town you have edited, pinned in or noted, so generating a new seed no longer loses one. "Here now" lists people by household. A Print button prints the inspector with all sections open and a map inset. The town's name goes where it covers the fewest roads and roofs.
- Serving: `town-gen-serve` runs `packaging/serve.py`, which revalidates the page on every load and caches hashed assets, so a rebuild never leaves a stale page. `tools/look.mjs` finds chromium-browser (Fedora) or takes `CHROME`.
